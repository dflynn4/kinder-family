The Kinder Family: Where Alan Comes From

Alan James Kinder was born in a coal-mining town in Washington State on January 26, 1925, and went to war in 1943 as a sound-ranging technician in Patton's Third Army. This is the family that made him: who his people were, where they came from, and how the paper actually reads.

A note on how to read this. Nothing here is copied off somebody's Ancestry tree and taken on faith. Every claim is graded. Proven means a real record backs it, a census page, a land patent, a death certificate, a marriage return. Probable means the evidence lines up but the one clinching document hasn't been pulled yet. Family story means that's what's been passed down or posted online, and it hasn't been proven. Where a popular online claim doesn't hold up, it's said so plainly.

Credit where it's due: the archives were swept by AI, the census indexes, the federal land records, the cemetery databases, the old pioneer rolls, and then the hits were checked by eye. Some of these records had not been looked at by anyone in a hundred years.

The Kinder Side

Alan's father's people

Oregon Trail pioneers, three lines of them. A Virginia boy who chased the Gold Rush, came back for a bride, and hauled her over the trail to claim free land in Oregon in 1852. His son was born on that land. The family then broke the wheat ground of the Walla Walla country, and four generations later Alan was in the snow at the Battle of the Bulge.

Paper trail: documented to Virginia around 1804, on the trail west in the 1850s, with a federal land patent as the anchor.

The Deye Side

Alan's mother's people

A German name out of Shelby County, Ohio, and an English-descended line with a Civil War corporal in it. The family drifted Ohio to Iowa to the Michigan lumber frontier, where Alan's mother was born, then out to Walla Walla, where the schoolteacher married the farmer.

Paper trail: documented to Ohio in 1834 and Indiana in the early 1800s, with a German immigrant just out of reach behind it.

The one-paragraph version

Alan's father's side is pure Oregon Trail: three separate families, the Kinders, the Evanses, and the Muncys, all crossed the plains to Oregon in the 1850s, and one of them, Franklin Kinder, did it the hard and interesting way, by way of the California Gold Rush and a wedding in Missouri, then claimed free government land in the Oregon Coast Range. The family moved north to the wheat country around Waitsburg, Washington, where Alan's father Darrell grew up. Alan's mother's side is a German family named Deye out of Ohio, mixed with an old English-descended North family from Indiana and Iowa that sent a corporal to the Civil War; they worked their way west through Michigan and landed in Walla Walla too, where Vera Deye, a schoolteacher, married Darrell Kinder, a farmer, in 1923. Their son Alan was born two years later. Pioneers and preachers and soldiers, and then a boy who went from a Washington coal town to the Third Army.
