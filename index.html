The Kinder Family: Where Alan Comes From

Alan James Kinder was born in a coal town in Washington State on January 26, 1925. In World War II he ran sound-ranging gear in Patton's Third Army. This is where his family came from, and what the old records actually say.

How to read this. Every fact here has a label:

  • ProvenThere's a real record for it. A census page, a land deed, a death certificate, a marriage license.
  • ProbableThe evidence points that way, but the one document that would nail it down hasn't turned up yet.
  • UnprovenIt's a guess. Usually something a stranger typed into an online family tree, with nothing real behind it.

One thing worth saying up front: nobody in the family remembers back this far. None of this is "what grandpa used to say." It's all dug out of paper. Where the paper runs out, it says so, and it doesn't pretend a guess is a fact.

And credit where it's due: a computer did the heavy lifting, reading through census rolls, old land records, cemetery listings, and pioneer lists, and then every hit was checked by eye. Some of these records hadn't been looked at by anyone in a hundred years.

Dad's Side: the Kinders

Alan's father's people

Oregon Trail pioneers, three families of them. A Virginia boy chased the Gold Rush, went back east to get married, and hauled his new wife over the trail to Oregon in 1852, where the government gave them free land. His son was born on that land. The family later moved to the wheat country of eastern Washington, and four generations on, Alan was in the snow at the Battle of the Bulge.

The paper goes back to Virginia around 1804, and there's a real federal land deed in the middle of it.

Mom's Side: the Deyes

Alan's mother's people

A German name out of the Ohio farm country, mixed with an old English-stock family that sent a soldier to the Civil War. They moved west a state at a time, Ohio to Iowa to the Michigan woods, where Alan's mother was born, and finally out to Walla Walla, where the schoolteacher married the farmer.

The paper goes back to Ohio in 1834, with a German immigrant just out of reach behind it.

The whole thing in a nutshell

Alan's dad's side is pure Oregon Trail. Three different families, the Kinders, the Evanses, and the Muncys, all crossed the plains to Oregon in the 1850s. One of them, Franklin Kinder, did it the interesting way: by way of the California Gold Rush and a wedding in Missouri, and then he claimed free government land in Oregon. The family later moved up to the wheat country around Waitsburg, Washington, where Alan's father grew up. Alan's mom's side is a German family named Deye out of Ohio, tied in with an English-stock family from Indiana and Iowa that had a Civil War soldier in it. They worked their way west through Michigan and landed in Walla Walla too. There, Vera Deye, a schoolteacher, married Darrell Kinder, a farmer, in 1923. Alan came along two years later. Pioneers, a preacher, and a soldier or two, and then a boy who went from a Washington coal town to the front line in Europe.
