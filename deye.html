Mom's Side: the Deyes

Alan's mother's people. An old New Jersey family with a Dutch-colonial name that settled the Ohio farm country, married into an English Quaker line with a Civil War soldier in it, and drifted west a state at a time until a schoolteacher married a farmer in Walla Walla.

The schoolteacher

Alan's mother was Vera May Deye. She was born around 1899 in Gladwin County, Michigan, up in the middle of the state Probable. By the time she was grown, the family had moved clear across the country to Walla Walla, Washington, where Vera became a schoolteacher. On October 15, 1923, she married a young wheat farmer from nearby Waitsburg named Darrell Kinder (his family is on the other page) Proven. Alan came along two years later. Vera lived a long life and died in 1995 at 96.

Their marriage license still exists, and it's a little goldmine, because on one page, in the couple's own answers, it confirms both families at once. It lists Darrell, farmer of Waitsburg, son of James B. Kinder and Ella Muncy. And it lists Vera, teacher, born in Michigan, daughter of Charles Deye of Ohio and Lou North of Iowa. Two whole generations, nailed down by one 1923 document.

Not a German name after all

Here's a correction worth being honest about. The easy assumption, and the one I made at first, was that "Deye" is a German name, because Vera's father came out of Shelby County, Ohio, a county full of German farmers. But when we actually chased the records back, the family turned out to be something else: an old New Jersey family of Dutch-colonial stock, not German at all.

Vera's father, Charles William Deye, was born on May 16, 1868, in Shelby County, Ohio, and lived until 1946 Proven. His father was Jacob Dey, born in Shelby County, Ohio, in 1834 Proven. Notice the spelling: the older gravestone reads "Dey," and the kids later softened it to "Deye."

And now we can name the man who brought the family to Ohio. Jacob's father was John Wesley Dey, born January 10, 1810, in New Jersey, who moved out to the Ohio farm country and died there in 1851 Probable. Three separate records point the same way, including his gravestone in the very same Ohio cemetery where his son Jacob is buried. The Dey family were part of the old Dutch settler stock of colonial New Jersey, the kind of family that had been in America since the 1600s, and they moved from New Jersey out to Ohio around 1830. (The internet trees try to run the name all the way back to Denmark in the 1500s. That deep stuff is unproven tree-copying, so it's left off. But the New Jersey roots are real.)

The other half: a Civil War soldier and a Quaker line

Vera's mother was Lou May North, born on October 15, 1876, in Hampton, Iowa (Vera's middle name, "May," came straight from her) Proven. This side reaches back the furthest of anyone on Alan's mom's side, and it has the best story on it.

Lou's father was Daniel Martin North, born on January 6, 1843, in Indiana Proven. He was a Civil War soldier, a corporal in Company H of the 32nd Iowa Infantry. That regiment saw brutal fighting: it was nearly surrounded and cut to pieces at the Battle of Pleasant Hill in Louisiana in 1864, fought at the Battle of Nashville that December, and finished the war helping storm the Confederate forts at Mobile, Alabama in April 1865 Proven. Daniel came home, farmed in Iowa, and raised his family there.

Daniel had a rough start himself: his mother, Maria Howe, died when he was only four months old, and he was raised by his grandmother Probable. His father was Layton North, born December 11, 1809, in Ohio, who married Maria in Indiana in 1838 and died young in 1851 Proven (his gravestone and marriage record both survive). And one more step back, probably: Layton's own parents look to be John North and Tamar Mendenhall Probable. "Mendenhall" is a well-known Quaker name, which fits, the Norths appear to be old English Quaker stock who came down the classic Quaker trail through the Carolinas and Ohio into Indiana.

The path west

Put it together and Alan's mom's family made the slow American drift, about one state per generation. The Deyes came from New Jersey to Ohio around 1830. Charles Deye then moved to Iowa, where he married Lou North, whose Quaker-stock family had worked their way over from Indiana. The young couple went up to the Michigan woods, where their daughters, Vera included, were born in the 1890s. And by the early 1900s the whole family had gone all the way out to Walla Walla, Washington. There, their schoolteacher daughter married into the Kinder wheat-ranching family, and Alan's two sides, the Oregon Trail Kinders and the New Jersey Deyes, finally met.

How far back it really goes

The Deye name is proven back to Jacob Dey, born 1834 in Ohio, and probably one more step to his father John Wesley Dey, born 1810 in New Jersey, of old Dutch-colonial stock. The North name is the deepest branch on this side: proven back to Layton North, born 1809 in Ohio, and probably one more step to a John North and Tamar Mendenhall, English Quakers. Both are honest, and both could go further with a little more digging.
