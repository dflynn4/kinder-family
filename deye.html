Mom's Side: the Deyes

Alan's mother's people. A German family named Deye out of the Ohio farm country, tied in with an old English-stock family that had a Civil War soldier in it. They moved west a state at a time, until a schoolteacher married a farmer in Walla Walla.

The schoolteacher

Alan's mother was Vera May Deye. She was born around 1899 in Gladwin County, Michigan, up in the middle of the state Probable. By the time she was grown, the family had moved clear across the country to Walla Walla, Washington, where Vera became a schoolteacher. On October 15, 1923, she married a young wheat farmer from nearby Waitsburg named Darrell Kinder (his family is on the other page) Proven. Alan came along two years later. Vera lived a long life and died in 1995 at 96.

Their marriage license still exists, and it's a little goldmine, because on one page, in the couple's own answers, it confirms both families at once. It lists Darrell, farmer of Waitsburg, son of James B. Kinder and Ella Muncy. And it lists Vera, teacher, born in Michigan, daughter of Charles Deye of Ohio and Lou North of Iowa. Two whole generations, nailed down by one 1923 document.

A German name out of Ohio

Deye is a German name. Vera's father, Charles William Deye, was born on May 16, 1868, in Shelby County, Ohio, and lived until 1946 Proven. That part of western Ohio was settled starting in the 1830s by German and Alsatian families who came in looking for cheap farmland.

The oldest Deye the records pin down is Charles's father, Jacob Dey, born in Shelby County, Ohio, in 1834 Proven. Notice the spelling: the older gravestone reads "Dey," and the kids later standardized it to "Deye." That kind of little change happens all the time with German names in America. Jacob married and farmed in Ohio his whole life.

Behind Jacob is the immigrant, the man who actually crossed the ocean, and this is where the free trail ends. Because Jacob was already born in Ohio in 1834, the immigrant was his father, a German who reached Ohio around 1832 Probable. His name isn't in the records I can get to for free yet, so for now he's real but unnamed, one step out of reach. The line is solid to Ohio in 1834, with Germany right behind it.

The other half: a Civil War soldier

Vera's mother was Lou May North, born on October 15, 1876, in Hampton, Iowa (Vera's middle name, "May," came straight from her) Proven. This side reaches back the furthest of anyone on Alan's mom's side.

Lou's father was Daniel Martin North, born on January 6, 1843, in Kokomo, Indiana Proven. He was a Civil War soldier, a corporal in Company H of the 32nd Iowa Infantry. After the war he farmed in Iowa. His own father, Layton North, born 1809, is named in the family record, which pushes this line back to Indiana in the early 1800s Probable.

The path west

Put it together and Alan's mom's family made the slow American drift, about one state per generation. The Deyes started in Ohio. Charles Deye moved to Iowa, where he married Lou North. The young couple then went up to the Michigan woods, where their daughters, Vera included, were born in the 1890s. And by the early 1900s the whole family had gone all the way out to Walla Walla, Washington. There, their schoolteacher daughter married into the Kinder wheat-ranching family, and Alan's two sides, the Oregon Trail Kinders and the German Deyes, finally met.

How far back it really goes

The Deye name is proven back to Jacob Dey, born 1834 in Ohio, with a German immigrant father just behind him, real but not yet named. The North name is proven back to Daniel North, born 1843 in Indiana, and probably one more step to Layton North around 1809. Both are honest, and both have a clear next move: one free census pull would likely name the German immigrant and confirm Vera's Michigan birth.
